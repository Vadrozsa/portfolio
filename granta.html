Overview

A smarter way to access grants in Hungary

NDA

This project was developed with a real Hungarian banking client and is covered by a non-disclosure agreement. Some details, data and screens are simplified or left out to protect confidential information.

Hungary has around 900,000 registered small and medium-sized businesses, and there are EU and state grants designed to help them grow. Yet many owners never apply. Not because they don't qualify, but because finding, understanding and completing an application is simply too hard.

Granta is a multilingual digital assistant that matches business owners to the grants they actually qualify for, explains them in plain language, and guides them all the way to submission.

Low awareness

According to EU survey data, only around a third of European SMEs are aware of the public financial support available to them (Flash Eurobarometer 441, 2016).

Grants are announced in calls written for experts, spread across many portals and providers, and rarely reach the owner of a small café or workshop directly.

Who we are designing for

The full spectrum of Hungarian SME owners

From local hospitality to manufacturing and independent services. They are experts in their own business, not in Hungarian grant law, and they don't have time to become one.

What we found

Four barriers stand between owners and the money

Low awareness

According to EU survey data, only around a third of European SMEs are aware of the public financial support available to them (Flash Eurobarometer 441, 2016).

No trusted guide

There is no single place that tells owners which grants fit them and what to do next, so many give up before they start.

Complex legal terms

Dense regulatory language creates confusion even for well-educated owners unfamiliar with Hungarian law.

Overwhelming paperwork

Multi-step applications with unclear requirements lead to abandonment before submission.

Synthesis · the current journey

Where owners drop off today

Mapping the barriers onto the steps of applying showed the real pattern: every step adds friction, and the experience gets worse just when effort is highest.

02Define

From many problems to one clear challenge

Looking at the four barriers together, we realised they were not four separate problems. They were one: the path from a business owner to the right grant is broken.

Problem statement

SME owners miss grants they already qualify for.

The money is there, but the path to it isn't: it is hidden, written in legal language, and broken into paperwork that nobody guides you through.

How might we…

Design principles

Four rules that guided every decision

01

Only show what fits

If a hard requirement fails, the grant disappears. A 0% match is misleading; absence from the list is cleaner.

02

Plain language first

Every requirement, deadline and amount explained without legal jargon, in the owner's own language.

03

Do the work for them

Build the eligibility profile from existing business data, so owners answer as few questions as possible.

04

Never a dead end

When a grant is not enough, connect owners to loans, guarantees and grant writers.

Goal

Help SME owners discover, understand and apply for the grants they qualify for, without needing an expert.

Grant matching logic

Two stages, so owners only see grants that truly fit

The core of the product is a rule system. We designed it so the interface could stay simple.

?

One question is always asked manually: “What do you want to use the funding for?” The investment goal can't be read from registry data, and it is what makes a match truly relevant.

Business case

Why this is a win-win

For Granta to exist, someone has to offer it. We framed it as a service a bank could provide to its SME clients.

User flow

Two ways in: with or without a VAT number

Not every owner has their company data at hand or they haven’t started the business yet. We split onboarding so those with a VAT number get a profile filled in for them, while others answer a few plain questions.

Main flow

Only the grants that fit, explained in plain terms

The home screen lists matching grants as cards. Each card answers the three questions owners care about first: what is it, how much, and by when.

1 2 3 4
  1. 1

    Proximity

    Grant name, provider, amount and deadline sit close together on each card, so they read as one unit of information.

  2. 2

    Similarity

    Every grant card shares the same structure and style, so owners can compare grants at a glance instead of reading each one.

  3. 3

    Common region

    Each card is its own container. The boundary separates one grant from the next, even in a long list.

  4. 4

    Figure–ground

    The mint call-to-action is the only saturated element on the card, so the next action is always obvious.

Freemium

Try it free, grow with the bank

A free version lets any owner set their language, build a business profile and explore the grants they match. It is also how the bank meets new SME clients.

Testing & validation

What we learned from users

We did a first round of testing with 5 Hungarian SME owners. The findings confirmed that this application would help SME owners to navigate and apply to grants with more confidence, because of the clear and understandable language and the application guide. The participants noted that it would also be great if Granta could gather the needed empty documents in one place and check for “basic” errors (typos and missing information) before the user uploads them to the official application site.

We also tested it with the bank client, and the feedback was positive.

Reflection

What this project taught me

What I learned

  • Designing the rules before the screens. The matching logic decided how simple the interface could be.
  • Designing to simplify complex legal terms is not easy and it requires precision.
  • Visual principles are a tool for clarity, not decoration. Gestalt helped us simplify dense, legal information.

Next steps

  • Test the matching logic and the plain-language copy with real SME owners.
  • Validate the bank handoff and freemium model with the bank.
  • Add more languages and an accessibility review.
